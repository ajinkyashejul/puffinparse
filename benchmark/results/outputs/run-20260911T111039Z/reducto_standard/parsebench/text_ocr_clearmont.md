'f someone mentions the Boston & Maine of the late steam era your ages of ancient Moguls trundling down a cinder ballasted right of way and rolling through wooden covered bridges. And so it was with the Clare- mont branch of the B&M right up until the mid 1950's. The Claremont branch was certainly neither the last B&M line to host steam nor was it the first B&M line to be sold off as a shortline, however, it was the best known of them all. Much has already been written about the line including an excellent book by Edgar T. Mead titled Through Covered Bridges To Concord (long out of print).

During the mid part of the century, the Boston & Maine decided that the Claremont branch was not a going con- cern and seriously worked toward the idea of giving up the operation. It was also about this time that shortline en- trepreneur Samuel Pinsly was starting his large collection of shortlines. Mr. Pinsly would eventually go on to own and operate six different former B&M or B&M owned operations; the Clare- mont Branch was among these.

Pinsly had already bought the little Claremont Railway and actively pur- sued the B&M's Claremont branch which would allow the two to be com- bined into one efficient operation. On December 17, 1954, the Claremont &

Concord Railway became official. In- cluded in the deal were the buildings, land and some equipment, which in- cluded one locomotive and a gas elec- tric passenger set.

Unfortunately, it was to be a shotgun wedding for the poor Claremont Rail- way. Within an incredibly short time period the wire came down from over the little electric road and most all of the equipment was dissected for the various metals and scrapped. It was decided to utilize the former B&M en- ginehouse at Claremont Junction rather than the Claremont Railway's carbarn for locomotive storage and re- pair. Soon enough the operation settled into a regular routine.
